# Education Market Opportunity — ASSET-FORGE "Turnkey Startup Packs" as Classroom Project Material

*Market memo — 2026-06-04. Markets in scope: Ireland (English), Slovakia (Slovak), with UK/EU as scale reference. Currency €.*

## Verdict

**Cautious-go, but reposition before you build.** A real and well-defined *audience* exists — entrepreneurship is formally taught and assessed at second level in both Ireland and Slovakia, and tens of thousands of students run mini-company / student-company projects every year [1][6][9]. The problem is **monetisation, not demand**: the dominant programmes that own this slot (Ireland's Student Enterprise Programme, Junior Achievement, UK's Young Enterprise, JA Slovensko) are run by state-backed/charity bodies and are delivered to schools **free or heavily subsidised** [1][5][6], and K-12 buyers default to a free-resource expectation under tight budgets [10]. The single biggest reason for caution: **the strongest segment (secondary schools) is also the one where willingness to pay a meaningful site licence is lowest, because an entrenched free incumbent already supplies the exact project students run.** The realistic paid wedge is **further/higher education and the "teacher convenience / authentic-artefact" niche**, not a broad K-12 site-licence play. Treat Education as a *secondary channel and credibility play*, not the core business — until a paid pilot proves a school will actually write a cheque.

## Demand & curriculum hooks

**Ireland (strongest curriculum fit).**
- **Junior Cycle Business Studies** has three strands — Personal Finance, **Enterprise**, Our Economy — with a group project ("Business in Action": research → evaluate → action plan → report) that maps almost one-to-one onto our pack structure [2].
- **Leaving Certificate Business** was revised with a new specification rolled out from September 2025 [2] — a content-refresh moment that creates openness to new resources.
- **Student Enterprise Programme** (run by the 31 Local Enterprise Offices): **~30,000+ students from ~500 secondary schools in 2024/25** (28k in 2022/23; 500k+ cumulative) — Ireland's largest student start-up programme, and structurally a head-on substitute since students already build a business plan/mini-company [1].
- **Transition Year mini-company** and **LCVP Enterprise Education** give a dedicated, low-stakes term-length slot for project material [2].
- **Junior Achievement Ireland**: 1.2M+ students since 1996, partnerships across ~165 sites; volunteer-delivered business/enterprise programmes [4].

**Slovakia (real, concentrated).**
- **JA Slovensko – Aplikovaná ekonómia / JA Firma**: students form a simulated joint-stock student company and run the full cycle (formation → market research → trading → liquidation with profit distribution). **51,763 students, 996 teachers, 796 schools in 2024/25**; 537k+ graduates over 33 years [6][7]. This is the single most direct analogue to our packs in the Slovak market — and again, it is an incumbent programme, not a gap.
- Slovakia has ~750–800 secondary schools (gymnáziá + stredné odborné školy), though many are sub-scale and enrolment-pressured [8] — a fragmented, low-budget buyer base.

**UK/EU (scale reference, not a launch market).**
- **Young Enterprise "Company Programme"** is the canonical paid analogue and the best pricing benchmark we found: **£560 per student company (state schools) / £2,500 (independent schools)** [5]. This proves schools *will* pay for a structured enterprise programme — but note it bundles mentoring, competitions and a national framework, not just files.
- **EntreComp** (EU JRC Entrepreneurship Competence Framework): 3 areas, 15 competences, 8 progression levels, **442 learning outcomes** [3]. This is the pan-EU spine to map an "Education Edition" against — it is the credibility currency procurement officers and curriculum leads recognise.

**Read-through:** demand is genuine and *named*, but in every core market the demand is already *served by a subsidised programme that does the same thing we do*. We are not filling a vacuum; we are trying to out-differentiate a free incumbent.

| Competitor / substitute | What it is | Indicative price | Notes |

| **JA Slovensko – Aplikovaná ekonómia (SK)** | Student-company programme, ~52k students/yr [6][7] | Free / sponsor-funded | Owns the SK slot |
| **Young Enterprise Company Programme (UK)** | Structured enterprise programme + mentoring | **£560/company state; £2,500 independent** [5] | Best paid benchmark; bundles more than files |
| **Junior Achievement (IE/SK/Europe)** | Volunteer-delivered enterprise modules [4][6] | Free / corporate-sponsored | Strong brand, free at point of use |
| **Teachers Pay Teachers** | Marketplace of teacher-made resources | **~$3–$60; bundles ~$52** [11] | Low ASP, English-only, no curriculum guarantee |

| **Twinkl** | Subscription resource library | School site licence by quote [12] | Primary-skewed; subscription, not project depth |
| **Capsim / MarketPlace / SimVenture** | Software business simulations | Capsim ~**$55/student** (older figure, unverified current) [13] | HE/exec market; software, not real workbooks |
| **Strategyzer / Kauffman BMC** | Business Model Canvas tools/templates | **Free (Creative Commons)** [14] | University default; free baseline to beat |

**Differentiation — honest read.** Our genuine edge is **authenticity**: real formula-driven workbooks (financial models, KPI dashboards, procurement trackers) and *real* funding templates (Irish LEO grants, Microfinance Ireland, SURE; Slovak ÚPSVaR/eurofondy). No competitor combines (a) production-grade Excel artefacts students could plausibly use for a *real* venture with (b) localised IE+SK grant/compliance content. TpT/TES resources are thin worksheets; simulations are black-box software; JA/Young Enterprise sell programme + mentoring, not deep modelling tooling. **But** that edge is also our weakness in K-12: real funding-application templates and full financial models are arguably *too complex* for the assessed Junior Cycle/SK secondary level, and the free incumbents are "good enough" for the curriculum requirement. The differentiation is sharpest exactly where willingness-to-pay is highest (FE/HE), and weakest where the audience is largest (secondary).

## Willingness to pay & how schools buy

- **K-12 reality: free is the anchor price.** Schools operate under tight budgets and are explicitly steered toward free/low-cost resources [10]; the flagship enterprise programmes are already free [1][6]. A paid site licence must clear a "why not just use the free LEO/JA programme?" objection on every call.
- **Procurement friction is high and slow.** Purchases route through department budgets and school/MAT approval; sales cycles align to the academic year (decisions in spring/summer for September). Expect long, low-velocity, relationship-led selling — not self-serve.
- **The paid benchmark that works** is Young Enterprise's **£560/student-company (state) and £2,500 (independent)** [5] — i.e., schools pay **hundreds, occasionally low-thousands**, when the offer is a *complete programme with external validation*, not loose files. Independent/fee-paying schools and universities are where four-figure deals live.
- **Licensing template:** TES's "3.5× single-licence = school multi-user licence" [12] is a clean, defensible site-licence multiple to copy.
- **HE/university budgets are larger and faster** (course/module budgets, enterprise-centre and incubator budgets, often grant-funded), but the buyer expects a polished, assessment-ready or simulation-grade product and already has free BMC/Lean tooling as the baseline [14].

## Segment ranking (best → worst fit)

1. **Universities / further education & colleges (best paid fit, smaller volume).** Entrepreneurship modules, enterprise centres, incubators/accelerators and BTEC/apprenticeship-style vocational courses can absorb *real* financial models and funding templates — exactly where our complexity is an asset, not a liability. Budgets are larger, procurement faster, and the "authentic artefact for a real venture" pitch lands. Caveat: the free Strategyzer/Kauffman BMC baseline [14] and incumbent simulations [13] set the bar.
2. **Secondary schools (biggest audience, hardest to monetise).** Largest, best-defined curriculum hooks (Junior Cycle Enterprise strand, Leaving Cert Business, TY mini-company, Student Enterprise Programme; SK Aplikovaná ekonómia) [1][2][6] — but a free, entrenched incumbent owns the exact slot and budgets resist paid resources [10]. Winnable only as a *premium, scaffolded, curriculum-mapped* convenience product, likely strongest in independent/fee-paying schools and with time-poor non-specialist teachers.
3. **Colleges / FE specifically for vocational business (mid).** BTEC/apprenticeship and SK SOŠ business tracks value real-world artefacts; budgets thin and fragmented. Decent fit, modest revenue.
4. **Primary schools (worst — drop for now).** Our packs (financial modelling, grant applications, procurement) are far above primary level; financial-literacy at primary is basic and well-served by free Twinkl-style material [12]. No realistic fit without rebuilding the product from scratch.

## What an "Education Edition" requires

Concrete adaptation checklist (do **not** ship the raw commercial packs into classrooms):
- **Scaffolded / tiered workbooks** — a simplified "student" tier (locked formulas, guided inputs, fewer tabs) and an "advanced/HE" tier that retains the full models.
- **Teacher pack**: lesson plans, term-length project structure (e.g. 12-week scheme of work), facilitation notes, and **group-work splits** (assign roles: finance lead, marketing, ops) for class delivery.
- **Assessment mapping**: rubrics + explicit alignment to **Junior Cycle Business Studies learning outcomes**, **Leaving Cert Business (2025 spec)**, SK ŠVP/Aplikovaná ekonómia, and **EntreComp's 442 learning outcomes / 8 levels** [2][3][6] — this is the procurement unlock.
- **Student worksheets / workbooks** and a student-facing project brief, plus a marking-ready submission template.
- **Localisation parity**: full Slovak-language edition; SK funding/compliance content (ÚPSVaR, eurofondy) at parity with the IE (LEO/MFI/SURE) content.
- **Licensing terms**: explicit classroom/site-licence covering N students/teachers per school per year, renewable annually; copy the TES 3.5× multi-user model [12].
- **Competition / showcase hook (optional but high-leverage)**: the reason Young Enterprise/SEP retain schools is the national final. We could partner into an existing programme rather than compete with it (see Risks).

## Recommended pricing & packaging

Grounded in Young Enterprise (£560 state / £2,500 independent) [5], TES 3.5× licensing [12], TpT bundle ASP (~$52) [11], and Capsim (~$55/student, unverified) [13]:

- **Single-classroom licence (one teacher, one cohort, one year): €250–€450.** Beats a TpT bundle on depth/localisation; below the YE state-school price.
- **School site licence (all business teachers, unlimited cohorts, one year): €900–€1,800 (state) / up to €3,000 (independent/fee-paying).** Anchored to YE and the 3.5× multi-user logic.
- **University / FE module licence: €1,500–€4,000 per module or department, annual** — justified by larger budgets and the full-model "advanced" tier; per-student pricing (~€20–€40) as an alternative where cohorts are large, undercutting Capsim's ~$55 [13].
- **Free "taster" tier** (one cut-down pack + one lesson plan) is essential to clear the free-resource expectation and seed adoption [10].

Realistic ceiling: with ~500 IE secondary schools running enterprise [1] and ~750 SK secondary schools [8], even optimistic single-digit-percent paid penetration at these prices is a **low-six-figure annual** ceiling in the two core markets — material as a *side channel*, not a company-maker. HE adds the larger per-deal upside.

## Risks & open questions

- **Free incumbent risk (highest).** SEP, JA, JA Slovensko and Young Enterprise occupy the exact slot, free or subsidised [1][5][6]. Mitigation: position as a **paid premium complement / "do-it-better" upgrade**, or — better — **partner/licence content into** one of these programmes rather than compete (e.g. supply the financial-modelling layer SEP/JA lack).
- **Complexity mismatch.** Our flagship assets (grant applications, full P&L/cashflow models) may overshoot Junior Cycle/secondary assessment level. Validate with 2–3 teachers before building.
- **Sales-cycle cost.** Academic-year procurement is slow and relationship-led; CAC may exceed licence revenue for K-12. The taster-tier + partnership route de-risks this.
- **Localisation cost.** A credible SK edition + curriculum mapping is real build effort for a market of fragmented, low-budget schools [8].
- **Open / unverified items:** current Capsim per-student price (2016 figure used) [13]; exact Twinkl/TES site-licence € figures (quote-based) [12]; precise split of TY-only vs all-year SEP participants [1]; whether the 2025 Leaving Cert Business spec names project artefacts we can map to directly [2]. Recommend a 5-school discovery round (3 IE secondary + 1 IE university + 1 SK gymnázium) and one call each with SEP/JA to test the **partnership-vs-compete** path before committing build budget.
